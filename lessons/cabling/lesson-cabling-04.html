MOD 04

Module 4: Crimping Theory & First Practice

Theme: Hands-On: Your First Cable

Learning Objectives

  • Hold a crimping tool and cable with correct technique
  • Execute a full crimp from strip to test-ready connector
  • Recognize the visual difference between a good and bad crimp
  • Explain the role of each crimping tool and the correct strip length

Safety Briefing (8:30–9:00)

  • Correct grip on the crimping tool
  • Correct way to hold the cable during stripping and insertion
  • Common mistakes: rushing, uneven pressure, wrong hand position
  • Safety focus: fingers, eyes (wire ends can flick), posture during repetitive motion
  • Inspect tools before use — a worn/dirty crimper gives weak crimps

Live Demonstration (9:00–10:30)

Instructor crimps a cable slowly, narrating every step:

Tools first — know what each is for (adapted from the field guide):

ToolPurpose
Crimp tool (crimper)Terminates RJ45 plugs with a ratcheting press
Cable stripperRemoves the outer jacket without nicking wires
Wire cutterTrims the bundle to exact length
Punch-down toolTerminates keystones/patch panels (110-style)
Cable testerVerifies continuity & pin order (mandatory before install)

The 7-step process:

  1. Strip 10–12mm of outer jacket — shear, don't nick the inner insulation
  2. Untwist and straighten the 4 pairs; keep twist as close to the jacket as possible (short untwisted run = low crosstalk)
  3. Arrange wires into T568B order: W-Orange, Orange, W-Green, Blue, W-Blue, Green, W-Brown, Brown
  4. Trim wires evenly to ~12–13mm past the jacket — all 8 flush
  5. Insert fully into the RJ45 connector (clip/tab down) — wires reach the front and the jacket passes the strain-relief barb
  6. Crimp with firm, even pressure in one motion until the ratchet releases
  7. Inspect: all 8 pins show copper contact, clip flush, no jacket trapped between conductors

Pause frequently to quiz students: "What color is pin 4?" Show a side-by-side comparison of a good crimp vs a bad crimp (loose wires, uneven length, connector not fully seated).

Lab 4A: Students' First Crimping (10:45–12:30)

  • Each student receives 2 blank cables + connectors
  • Instructor circulates, watching grip and pressure
  • Cable 1: focus purely on technique, not speed
  • Cable 2: try to improve on Cable 1
  • No testing yet — that comes next week
  • Reassure students: "It's OK if your first one fails"

Lab 4B: Practice Video + Feedback (1:15–2:15)

  • Watch a video highlighting common crimping mistakes
  • Instructor gives individual feedback per student
  • Group discussion: what felt different between Cable 1 and Cable 2? (tool pressure, wire arrangement, insertion depth)
  • Review the common-mistakes table from the resource guide and check each student's work against it

Wrap-Up (2:15–2:30)

Students keep their two cables. Preview: "Next week we test them." Reinforce: even pressure + wire depth + twist length are the three habits that separate reliable cables from interrogators' rejects.

Check for Understanding

  1. What is the correct strip length before crimping?
  2. Name two visual signs of a bad crimp.
  3. Why is even pressure important during the crimp?
  4. Name the two tools you must use before and after crimping to ensure a reliable cable.

Home Assignment

  • Bring 4 more blank cables for Saturday 5
  • Practice the finger grip motion without a cable
  • Re-watch the crimping video twice
  • Note: don't test your cables yet — testing is next session
