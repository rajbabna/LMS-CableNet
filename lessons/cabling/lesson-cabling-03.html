Module 3: RJ45 Connectors & Wiring Standards

Theme: The RJ45 Connector: Your Crimp Target

Learning Objectives

  • Identify the 8 pins of an RJ45 connector and their purpose
  • Recall the T568B wiring order from memory
  • Explain the difference between T568A and T568B
  • Explain when a straight-through vs crossover cable is needed
  • Describe the real pair-to-pin circuits (1-2, 3-6, 4-5, 7-8) and why order matters

Quick Recall Quiz (8:30–9:00)

Open with a rapid pin-order recall check from last week's homework.

Lecture Notes (9:00–10:30)

8 pins, numbered 1–8 left to right when the clip faces down/away from you. Three physical parts matter for the crimp:

  • 8 gold contact pins — each pierces one conductor
  • Transparent body/housing — lets you verify colors before crimping
  • Locking tab — clicks into the port/keystone so the plug stays seated

> Always inspect the plug: a pin not aligned with its conductor can test > "up" but fail under PoE or high throughput.

2Orange
3White/Green
4Blue

Ethernet does NOT use pins in simple 1..8 order. The cable is 4 twisted pairs, and the usable circuit pairs are:

  • 1-2, 3-6, 4-5, 7-8

Only pairs 1-2 and 3-6 are needed for 100 Mbps; all four pairs (8 conductors) are used for Gigabit. This is why "pins 3 and 6 are the green pair" — they are not physically adjacent, and getting them right is the single most common cause of dead cables.

Swaps the orange and green pairs relative to T568B (pins 1↔3, 2↔6). The only difference between the two standards is swapping pairs 2 and 3.

  • Straight-through (T568B both ends, or T568A both ends) — connects unlike devices, e.g., PC to switch. This is what students will make ~90% of the time.
  • Crossover (T568A one end, T568B the other) — historically used to connect like devices directly (PC to PC, switch to switch). Modern switches auto-detect (auto-MDIX), but understanding crossover is still tested and useful for troubleshooting older equipment.

Wrong pin order can mean partial connectivity, wrong speed negotiation, or a completely dead cable — this is the #1 cause of "the cable doesn't work" in the field. Pick ONE standard (T568B) and use it consistently on both ends. Consistency matters more than which standard you choose.

Lab 3A: RJ45 Connector Anatomy (10:45–11:30)

  • Examine RJ45 connectors with a magnifier
  • Identify the 8 pin contacts and where each wire seats
  • Discuss: why does order matter physically, not just electrically?
  • Note where the strain-relief barb is and why the jacket must pass it

Lab 3B: Pin Order Practice (11:30–12:30)

  • Using a laminated diagram, arrange colored wires in T568B order
  • Practice with dummy (non-terminated) wire sets first
  • Repeat the sequence 5 times to build muscle memory — frame it as "color sequence is muscle memory," not just memorization
  • Bonus challenge: arrange T568A and note exactly which two pairs move

Lab 3C: Pre-Crimping Preparation (1:15–2:15)

  • Review cable construction diagram (jacket, pairs, conductors)
  • Demonstrate proper cable hold and hand position
  • Show stripping measurement: 10–12mm of jacket removed — no nicking the insulation of the inner conductors
  • Explain conductor pairs stay twisted as close to the plug as possible
  • Safety briefing on crimping tools (no live crimping yet — that's next week)
  • Discuss failure modes in advance: uneven wire length, wires not fully seated, jacket stripped too far back

Check for Understanding

  1. Write the T568B pin order from memory (colors 1–8)
  2. When would you use a crossover cable instead of straight-through?
  3. What's the correct strip length before crimping?
  4. What are the four usable pair circuits, and why are 1-2 and 3-6 the key pairs?
