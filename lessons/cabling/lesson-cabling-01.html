MOD 01

Module 1: OSI Model & Network Topologies

Theme: Understanding the Network Foundation

Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, students will be able to:

  • Name and describe the 7 layers of the OSI model
  • Explain what happens at each layer in plain language
  • Identify and compare Bus, Star, Ring, and Mesh topologies
  • Recommend a topology for a given real-world scenario

Lecture Notes (9:00–10:30)

The OSI (Open Systems Interconnection) model breaks network communication into 7 layers, from the physical wire up to the application a user sees:

  1. Physical — cables, connectors, voltages, radio signals
  2. Data Link — MAC addresses, switches, framing
  3. Network — IP addresses, routers, routing
  4. Transport — TCP/UDP, ports, reliable delivery
  5. Session — establishing/maintaining connections
  6. Presentation — encryption, data formatting
  7. Application — what the user interacts with (browser, email)

Teaching tip: use the mnemonic "Please Do Not Throw Sausage Pizza Away" to help students recall the order bottom-up.

This course lives mostly at Layer 1 (Physical) and touches Layer 2 (switches) — but understanding the full stack helps students see where their cabling work fits into the bigger picture.

TopologyLayoutProsCons
BusSingle shared cableCheap, simpleOne break kills the network
StarAll devices to central switchEasy to troubleshoot, most common todaySwitch is single point of failure
RingDevices connected in a loopPredictable traffic flowOne break can disrupt the ring
MeshEvery device connects to many othersHighly redundantExpensive, complex cabling

Emphasize: Star topology is what students will build in almost every lab in this course — it's the real-world default.

Lab 1: Topology Design Exercises (10:45–12:30)

  1. Draw a LAN for a small office building (10–15 devices)
  2. Draw a topology for a hospital network (redundancy matters — patient safety systems can't go down)
  3. For each scenario below, have students pick and justify a topology: - A single classroom with 20 laptops - A hospital ICU - A budget internet café
  4. Use paper/whiteboard or a simple drawing app — no software required yet

Afternoon Activity (1:15–2:15)

  • Watch a short cable basics video (10 min)
  • Group discussion: "Where do you see networks in daily life?" (home WiFi, school computer labs, ATMs, mobile networks)
  • Open Q&A

Check for Understanding

  1. What layer of the OSI model do Ethernet cables operate at?
  2. Why is Star topology more common today than Bus?
  3. Name one advantage and one disadvantage of Mesh topology.

Home Assignment

  • Read the cabling standards handout (2 pages)
  • Watch the "Ethernet cable basics" video (10 min)
  • Identify and photograph/list the cables in your own home or workplace
