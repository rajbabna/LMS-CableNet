## Multiple Choice

**1.** Why are copper pairs twisted inside a network cable?

- A. To make the cable flexible
- B. To cancel electromagnetic interference (EMI)
- C. To reduce the cost of copper
- D. To increase the cable's length limit

**2.** Which UTP category supports 10 Gbps up to 100 m and is the recommended future-proof choice for new structured installs?

- A. Cat5e
- B. Cat6
- C. Cat6a
- D. Cat8

**3.** A cable with a foil/braid shield around the twisted pairs is called:

- A. UTP
- B. STP
- C. Coaxial
- D. Fiber

**4.** What is the practical reach limit of copper UTP for LAN runs?

- A. 10 m
- B. 100 m
- C. 500 m
- D. 40 km

**5.** Which standard governs commercial building cabling?

- A. IEEE 802.3
- B. ISO 9001
- C. EIA/TIA-568
- D. RFC 1918

**6.** Which cable family is immune to electrical interference and best for runs over 100 m between buildings?

- A. UTP Cat5e
- B. STP Cat6
- C. Fiber optic
- D. Coaxial

---

## Short Answer

**7.** What does "UTP" stand for, and when would you choose STP instead?

**8.** What is "structured cabling," and why does labeling matter in a real building?

**9.** Name one building-code/safety rule that applies when running cable above ceiling tiles.

---

## Answer Key

1. **B** — Twisting cancels EMI/crosstalk from adjacent pairs and the environment (§1, §Key Takeaways).
2. **C** — Cat6a: 10 Gbps at 100 m, 500 MHz, recommended future-proof choice (§2).
3. **B** — STP (Shielded Twisted Pair) (§1, module §UTP vs STP).
4. **B** — ~100 m (§1 cable hierarchy table).
5. **C** — EIA/TIA-568 (§6 in resource, module §Standards).
6. **C** — Fiber: immune to EMI, 100 m – 40 km+ (§1).
7. UTP = Unshielded Twisted Pair — standard office/home use. Choose STP near heavy electrical interference (factories, power lines) (module §UTP vs STP).
8. Structured cabling = planned, labeled, standardized cable runs instead of ad-hoc wiring; labeling keeps installs maintainable (module §Standards).
9. Plenum/fire rating required above ceiling tiles; or never run parallel to mains power for long distances (resource §6).

> Difficulty: recall (Q1–6), explain/apply (Q7–9).
